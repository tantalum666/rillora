## Correction To The Earlier Framing

Owner challenges the earlier cautious conclusion that company logos should be usable. That challenge is justified as a distinction: absence of a general vendor license does not by itself establish that all factual references are unlawful. A commercial app can contain independent instructional content; it is not automatically advertising or co-branding. Conversely, a right to name a company does not automatically clear every graphic, copyrighted asset, contract or jurisdiction.

Hong Kong's Trade Marks Ordinance section21 expressly addresses using a mark to identify the owner's or licensee's goods/services, subject to honest industrial/commercial practices. It names unfair advantage, harm to distinctiveness/reputation and public deception as relevant considerations. This is a trademark rule, not an automatic copyright permission or a guarantee for every launch country. Read the [current WIPO record](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/23733) (2025 version, amended to L.N.171/2024); the English verified PDF's section21 was downloaded, text-read and visually inspected in this QA.

The [U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/engage/visual-artists/) also distinguishes simple word logos/minimal art that may lack copyright protection from possible trademark protection. We do not use that general statement to certify individual company artwork as public domain.

## Exact Rillora Context

- Small, secondary identifiers next to a named platform's independent preparation/deceased-account guidance, with direct official-source links.
- Public reading plus matching guidance beside an owner or recipient matter; no provider payment button, sign-in claim, technical integration badge, sponsor list or partnership lock-up.
- Do not put provider marks into Rillora's name, app icon, advertising, paid-plan promotion or owned logo.
- Use current official files, preserve colors and proportions and provide clear space. Do not recolor all brands to Rillora sage or synthesize approximate logos.
- Preserve attribution, source and non-affiliation information. Treat each expansion to a different use context as a new question.

## Deeper Practice Review — Current Adoption Method

Owner asks why many other apps/sites use these marks. The [follow-up research](provider-icon-practice-research.md) checks Bitwarden's official documentation and pinned icon-fetching source, 1Password's rich-icon documentation, KeePassXC's explicit favicon-download behavior and Home Assistant's brand repository. These are functional website/account/service identifiers, not merely partnership logo walls. Their unpublished contracts remain unknown.

Assess three different placements: a user's Matter account identifier, the subject of independent guidance, and marketing/co-branding or actual sign-in/payment/integration controls. Do not impose a separate written vendor agreement as the only possible basis for every small truthful account reference. Published permission and a reasoned functional-reference assessment under applicable law are distinct routes; neither inherits the other route's proof. Specific restrictions, independent copyright/contract issues and App Review remain part of the exact asset/context review. This is a provisional implementation method, not a declaration that all pending marks are now cleared.

For a candidate, record exact product/brand, original first-party source and hash, placement, date, basis (`published-permission`, `functional-reference-assessment`, `specific-agreement` or `unresolved`), applicable conditions and adopted state. A favicon is an intentional site identifier under HTML semantics, but its format alone grants no new legal rights. Do not borrow an integration/sign-in badge to imply a relationship that Rillora does not have.

Keep adopted artwork original and secondary to accurate text. Use the small fixed catalog locally: native bundled assets and first-party Web static resources. Do not issue third-party icon lookups from private Matter domains or infer B's icon from hidden A fields. This privacy rule follows the existing disclosure contract and the documented domain leakage of remote icon services. Do not add runtime icon infrastructure for this research checkpoint.

The table below records the **current implementation action**, not a blanket legal blacklist. New functional-reference candidates can be evaluated without reopening a general commercial-logo prohibition. The preceding practice-research checkpoint added no runtime asset; Owner's following instruction to use as many supportable marks as possible now adds Coinbase under the source/basis record below.
